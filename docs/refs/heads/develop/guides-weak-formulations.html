Finite-strain models

Linearized strain assumes small deformation. Finite-strain mechanics uses the deformation gradient and a constitutive energy, from which an internal virtual-work residual and consistent tangent are obtained. The solid mechanics guide explains that construction; the residual and its consistent tangent must represent the chosen nonlinear model.

Complex and mixed formulations

Over complex fields, the usual finite element convention is trial-linear and test-conjugate-linear. For Helmholtz, this gives

Positive definiteness is checked from the coefficients, boundary conditions and spectrum of the form. The Helmholtz tutorial uses an indefinite system and checks a manufactured solution.

Mixed equations introduce coupled unknowns and constraints. In Stokes flow, velocity and pressure form a saddle-point system; compatible spaces and a pressure gauge are needed. In Navier–Stokes, convection adds a nonlinear dependence that must be discretized in time and linearized explicitly.

Numerical quadrature and approximated geometry can introduce additional errors beyond the field approximation.

A small algebraic residual establishes that the assembled system was solved accurately. Model accuracy and spatial discretization error are assessed separately. See the form-language guide for assembly, solvers for algebraic checks, and numerical contracts for implementation rules.
