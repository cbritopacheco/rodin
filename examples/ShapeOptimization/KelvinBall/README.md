# Kelvin ball

A rigid body moving through a viscous fluid at vanishing Reynolds number
responds to a force and a torque through one linear map, its **grand resistance
tensor**. A **Kelvin ball** is a body for which pushing it also makes it turn:
its translation–rotation coupling is as strong as its translational and
rotational resistances allow. This example searches for such bodies by level-set
shape optimisation, following the notation of *Shape optimisation of the Kelvin
ball via a level-set method*.

It builds two executables:

- `KelvinBall` — the optimisation loop.
- `KelvinBallSphere` — a validation tool that evaluates the objective on the
  symmetry-reduced chamber and on the full domain, and compares the two.

## The problem

### Geometry and Stokes states

Let $D \subset \mathbb R^3$ be the container, $\Omega_{\mathrm{s}} \Subset D$ the
body, $\Omega_{\mathrm{f}} := D \setminus \overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}}$ the fluid,
and $\Gamma := \partial\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}$ the interface, with unit normal $n$
pointing from the body into the fluid.

The body moves rigidly, with a velocity field in

```math
\mathcal W := \{\, U(x) = Z + \omega \times x \;:\; Z,\ \omega \in \mathbb R^3 \,\},
```

where $Z$ is the translational and $\omega$ the angular velocity. Writing
$e(w) := \tfrac12(\nabla w + \nabla w^\top)$ and
$\sigma(w, r) := 2\mu\, e(w) - r I$, the **state** generated by $U \in \mathcal W$
is the Stokes flow $(u, p)$ solving

```math
\begin{cases}
-\mu \Delta u + \nabla p = 0 & \text{in } \Omega_{\mathrm{f}}, \\
\mathrm{div}\, u = 0 & \text{in } \Omega_{\mathrm{f}}, \\
u = U & \text{on } \Gamma, \\
u = 0 & \text{on } \partial D.
\end{cases}
```

The body is dragged by $U$ and the container wall is at rest.

### The resistance tensor

The force and torque needed to sustain the motion depend linearly on
$(Z, \omega)$:

```math
\begin{bmatrix} F \\ T \end{bmatrix}
=
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} K & C \\ C^\top & Q \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathcal R(\Omega_{\mathrm{s}})}
\begin{bmatrix} Z \\ \omega \end{bmatrix},
\qquad K,\ C,\ Q \in \mathbb R^{3\times 3}.
```

$K$ resists translation, $Q$ resists rotation, and the coupling block $C$ says
how much translating the body makes it turn.

### What the symmetry buys

The body is required to be invariant under the rotation group
$\mathcal G = \mathcal O$ of the cube, $|\mathcal G| = 24$. This group acts
irreducibly on $\mathbb R^3$, so each block collapses to a multiple of the
identity,

```math
K = k I, \qquad C = c I, \qquad Q = q I, \qquad
k := \tfrac13 \mathrm{tr}\, K,\quad c := \tfrac13 \mathrm{tr}\, C,\quad q := \tfrac13 \mathrm{tr}\, Q ,
```

and the whole resistance of the body is carried by three numbers.

Only proper rotations are imposed. A mirror symmetry would force $c = 0$: the
angular velocity is a pseudovector, so a reflection flips the sign of the
coupling. **A Kelvin ball must therefore be chiral.**

### The objective

The coupling is normalised against the resistances the same body already has,

```math
\rho(\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}) := \frac{|c|}{\sqrt{k\,q}} \in [0, 1),
```

which makes it scale invariant; the bound follows from positive definiteness of
$\mathcal R$. The optimisation problem is

```math
\max \bigl\{\, \rho(\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}) \;:\; \Omega_{\mathrm{s}}\ \mathcal G\text{-invariant},\ \ |\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}| = |\Omega^0_{\mathrm{s}}| \,\bigr\}.
```

## The shape derivative

By Hadamard's structure theorem, the derivative of a shape functional $J$ in the
direction of a deformation $\theta$ depends only on the normal motion of the
interface, $dJ(\Omega_{\mathrm{s}})[\theta] = \int_\Gamma g_J\,(\theta\cdot n)\,dS$.
Tangential motion merely re-parametrises $\Gamma$.

Each family of rigid data is solved as a triple: the translations
$U^T_i := e_i$ and the rotations $U^R_i := e_i \times x$, with states
$u^T_i$ and $u^R_i$, for $i = 1, 2, 3$. The densities of the three coefficients
are

```math
g_k = \frac{2\mu}{3}\sum_{i=1}^3 e(u^T_i) : e(u^T_i), \qquad
g_c = \frac{2\mu}{3}\sum_{i=1}^3 e(u^T_i) : e(u^R_i), \qquad
g_q = \frac{2\mu}{3}\sum_{i=1}^3 e(u^R_i) : e(u^R_i),
```

and the chain rule gives the density of the objective,

```math
g_\rho = \frac{\mathrm{sign}(c)}{\sqrt{k q}}\, g_c
       - \frac{\rho}{2}\left( \frac{g_k}{k} + \frac{g_q}{q} \right).
```

No adjoint state is needed: the six states that evaluate $\rho$ also give its
derivative. On the chamber each density carries the factor $|\mathcal G|$, as
the coefficients themselves do.

The derivative requires $c \neq 0$. The example starts from the unit sphere,
for which $c$ vanishes in the continuum; on the chamber discretisation $c$ is
small but not zero, and its sign selects the first ascent direction.

## The update

### Hilbert extension and the volume constraint

The density $g_\rho$ lives on $\Gamma$. It is turned into a smooth deformation
field on the whole container by the Riesz representation in $\mathcal H$, the
closure of the equivariant fields in $H^1_0(D;\mathbb R^3)$ with inner product

```math
\langle \theta, \eta \rangle_{\mathcal H} = \int_D \bigl( \ell^2 \nabla\theta : \nabla\eta + \theta\cdot\eta \bigr)\,dx,
\qquad \ell = 4h .
```

The length multiplier is set by `--regularization` (default 4).

This gives $\nabla\rho$ and $\nabla\mathcal V$, the gradients of the objective
and of the volume $\mathcal V(\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}) = |\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}|$, whose
density is $g_{\mathcal V} = 1$. The null-space method of Feppon, Allaire and
Dapogny keeps only the part of $\nabla\rho$ that does not change the volume:

```math
\lambda^* = -\frac{d\mathcal V[\nabla\rho]}{d\mathcal V[\nabla\mathcal V]},
\qquad
\xi_\rho = \nabla\rho + \lambda^* \nabla\mathcal V,
\qquad\text{so that}\qquad d\mathcal V[\xi_\rho] = 0 .
```

The coefficient $\lambda^*$ is evaluated with the assembled volume differential,
so the discrete direction satisfies $d\mathcal V[\xi_\rho] = 0$ to roundoff.
Volume changes caused by the finite step, transport and reconstruction are
reported at the next iterate; there is no range-space correction and no line
search.

### Transport

The body is the negative region of a level set $\phi$,
$\Omega_{\mathrm{s}} = \{\phi < 0\}$ and $\Gamma = \{\phi = 0\}$, starting from
$\phi_0(x) = |x| - 1$. The direction is normalised in the nodal maximum norm,

```math
\widehat\theta^{\,n} = \frac{\xi_{\rho,h}}{\|\xi_{\rho,h}\|_{L^\infty}},
```

so that the step length, not the metric, sets the size of the update. The level
set is transported for a time $\tau = 0.1\,h$ (`--step`) by

```math
\partial_t \Phi + \widehat\theta^{\,n} \cdot \nabla\Phi = 0, \qquad \Phi(0,\cdot) = \phi^n ,
```

and redistanced before each transport.

## The fundamental chamber

Every quantity above lives on the whole fluid domain, which consists of 24
congruent copies. The computation uses one of them, a **fundamental chamber**
$D_{\mathrm{c}}$ with $\overline D = \bigcup_{G \in \mathcal G} G\,\overline{D_{\mathrm{c}}}$,
and recovers the rest by symmetry.

The chamber is bounded by the physical outer surface and by artificial **cuts**
$\Sigma$, which come in pairs $(\Sigma_{\mathrm{s}}, \Sigma_{\mathrm{m}})$ related by a
rotation, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{m}} = G\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{s}}$. On the whole domain the states
of one family satisfy

```math
\mathsf U(Gx) = G\,\mathsf U(x)\,G^\top, \qquad \mathsf P(Gx) = G\,\mathsf P(x),
```

where $\mathsf U = [u_1\ u_2\ u_3]$ collects the three velocities and
$\mathsf P = (p_1, p_2, p_3)^\top$ the pressures. The left factor rotates each
velocity; the right factor mixes the three states of the family. The condition
on a cut therefore couples all three states, and **each family is solved as one
coupled system** rather than three independent ones.

The resistance is the chamber's viscous dissipation times the number of copies,
for instance

```math
K_{ij} = |\mathcal G|\; 2\mu \int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{f}}} e(u^T_i) : e(u^T_j)\,dx .
```

With $D = [-L, L]^3$ and $L$ = `--outer-radius` (default 2), sewing the 24
chambers gives the cube. The mesh attributes are:

| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `2` | Body $\Omega_{\mathrm{s}}$ |
| `3` | Fluid $\Omega_{\mathrm{f}}$ |
| `7` | Outer container $\partial D$ |
| `13` | Interface $\Gamma$ |
| `31`, `32`, `33`, `35` | Chamber cuts $\Sigma$ |

## Discretisation

### Stokes on the chamber

Velocity and pressure are both continuous piecewise linear, $V_h \times Q_h$.
This pair is not inf–sup stable, so a pressure-gradient term is added. For one
state the bulk form is

```math
a_h\bigl((u_h,p_h),(v_h,q_h)\bigr)
= 2\mu\int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{f}}} e(u_h):e(v_h)
- \int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{f}}} p_h \mathrm{div}\, v_h
- \int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{f}}} q_h \mathrm{div}\, u_h
- \sum_{K} \delta_K \int_K \nabla p_h\cdot\nabla q_h,
\qquad
\delta_K = \alpha\,\frac{h_K^2}{\mu},
```

with $\alpha$ = `--stabilization` (default 0.05). The code uses the local size
$h_K$ of each cell, the edge of the regular tetrahedron of the same volume, where
the paper states a single global $h$. The two agree on a quasi-uniform mesh; on
the graded meshes produced by reconstruction the local size matches the inverse
estimate that the term is derived from.

The rigid data and the no-slip wall are imposed strongly. On the cuts, where
the two triangulations need not match, the rotated condition is imposed weakly
by a symmetric Nitsche form on the jumps

```math
[\mathsf U_h]_G(y) = \mathsf U_h(y) - G\,\mathsf U_h(x)\,G^\top,
\qquad
[\mathsf P_h]_G(y) = \mathsf P_h(y) - G\,\mathsf P_h(x),
\qquad y = Gx \in \Sigma_{\mathrm{m}} ,
```

with penalty $\gamma\mu/h_\Sigma$ and $\gamma$ = `--penalty` (default 320). A
point of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{s}}$ is rotated onto $\Sigma_{\mathrm{m}}$ and located there
with an AABB tree. The pressure stabilisation is transmitted across the cuts in
the same way.

No pressure gauge is added: a constant pressure $\kappa$ would have to satisfy
$\kappa = G\kappa$ for every $G$, and irreducibility leaves only $\kappa = 0$.

Each family is one symmetric indefinite system. It is solved with MUMPS as an
$LDL^\top$ factorisation when Rodin is configured with `RODIN_USE_MUMPS`, and
otherwise with UMFPACK or Eigen's SparseLU. Forms are assembled with OpenMP when
`RODIN_USE_OPENMP` is set. The selected backends are printed at startup and
recorded in `kelvin-ball.csv`.

### Transport on the chamber

The level set is invariant rather than equivariant, $\phi(Gx) = \phi(x)$, so its
jump across a pair of cuts is $[r]_G(x) = r(Gx) - r(x)$. The Eikonal distance is
projected into $W_h$ with the penalty

```math
j_h(r, s) = \gamma_\phi\, h_\Sigma \int_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{s}}} [r]_G\,[s]_G\,dS ,
```

with $\gamma_\phi$ = `--penalty`, so that the two sides of each cut carry the
same distance before transport. A characteristic that reaches a cut continues
from its rotated partner, and the semi-Lagrangian update carries the same
penalty on both sides, so a zero step leaves $\phi$ unchanged.

The transported distance is integrated with order 8 by default
(`--advection-quadrature`); the composition with the characteristic flow is not
polynomial, so a low order under-integrates it.

## Reconstruction

After each transport the new zero level set must be resolved by the mesh
again. Two methods are available through `--reconstruction`.

### MMG (default)

MMG discretises the advected level set at every iterate, with
$h_{\mathrm{min}} = 0.1\,h$, $h_{\mathrm{max}} = 10\,h$, a Hausdorff tolerance of
$0.1\,h^2$ and gradation 2, followed by an optimisation pass with the same
settings. The outer boundary and the cuts are required geometry, and their
labels and planarity are checked after every reconstruction.

### WNGIR

MMG is called **once**, before any interface exists, to optimise the
interface-free chamber. That mesh is the fixed background for the whole run;
its settings are exposed as `--background-hmin`, `--background-hmax` and
`--background-hausdorff` (in multiples of $h$, defaults 0.1, 1 and 0.05) and
`--background-gradation` (default 2).

At every iterate the level set labels the background cells, which selects an
envelope of internal facets, and WNGIR fits a fresh copy of the background so
that this envelope reaches the zero level set. The fitted copy carries the
state and shape computations and is never reused as a background.

The boundary is treated by what it must preserve:

- the outer sphere is curved, so its nodes are pinned, $u = 0$;
- the cuts only have to stay planes, so their nodes slide within them,
  $u \cdot n = 0$. This is imposed exactly by eliminating the normal
  displacement; nodes on the intersection of two cuts slide along their common
  line. Pinning the cuts instead would freeze the rim of the interface, which
  lies entirely on them.

The distance and the direction live on the fitted mesh, whose vertices have
moved. They are carried to the background by evaluation at the background's
own positions, which keeps the fitted interface in the transported level set.

The level-set gradient is first projected into continuous P1 vectors, so its
trace on the envelope is unambiguous. A fit stops once the root-mean-square
distance from the envelope to the zero level set, relative to the mesh scale,
drops below a tolerance. That tolerance is at least `--wngir-rms-floor`
(default 0.005) and grows with the normal jump across the skeleton. The default
budget is `--wngir-steps=12` and the default bulk coefficient on this path is
`--wngir-kappa-bulk=8e-4`. The other fitting parameters use the common
`--wngir-*` spellings of the WNGIR examples (see `KelvinBall --help`).

## Running

```sh
KelvinBall --n=17 --iterations=20
KelvinBall --h=0.125 --iterations=20
KelvinBall --outer-radius=3 --h=0.1666666667 --iterations=2
KelvinBall --n=25 --iterations=5 --reconstruction=wngir
```

The resolution is given either as points per edge (`--n`) or as a mesh size
(`--h`, rounded up); the effective size is $h = L/(n-1)$. Without arguments the
example uses `--n=13 --outer-radius=2 --iterations=1 --penalty=320
--stabilization=0.05 --regularization=4 --step=0.1`. `--geometry-only` stops
after the initial reconstruction and `--state-only` after the first Stokes
evaluation.

Quick checks:

```sh
KelvinBall --n=9 --iterations=1 --step=0.01    # one evaluation, outputs only
KelvinBall --n=13 --iterations=2 --step=0.01   # one geometry update
```

`KelvinBallSphere` evaluates $\rho$ on the chamber and on the full domain sewn
from the 24 rotated chambers, where each state carries one mean-pressure
constraint, and prints both with their relative difference. The sewn mesh is
written to `KelvinBallSphere.xdmf`.

```sh
KelvinBallSphere --n=13 --outer-radius=2
KelvinBallSphere --h=0.125 --outer-radius=2 --save-mesh
```

## Output

Each iterate is written to three XDMF series.

- `KelvinBall.xdmf` — the chamber design and states. On iterations followed by
  an update, `Advected` is the transported distance on the mesh passed to the
  reconstruction.
- `KelvinBallSewed.xdmf` — the complete 24-copy design with the sewn distance
  and deformation, and a complete fluid grid carrying the six sewn velocities
  and pressures.
- `KelvinBallMMG.xdmf` or `KelvinBallWNGIR.xdmf` — the reconstructed mesh,
  before the next finite-element spaces are built.

`kelvin-ball.csv` has one row per design: run parameters, mesh and material
counts, tetrahedron quality, the reconstruction settings, $k$, $c$, $q$, $\rho$,
the volume error, the predicted and realised changes of $\rho$ and of the
volume, the direction derivatives, the extension residuals and jumps, and the
wall-clock time of each stage. Fields that do not exist for the initial design
or a `--state-only` run are `nan`.
